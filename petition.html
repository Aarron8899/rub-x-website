🌍 How Can We Punish Those Who Solve Our Problems?

📌 Who is solving problems for whom?

Worldwide, millions of rubber tappers endure dangerous, repetitive night work to meet global demand for tires, medical devices, and everyday products. In return, they suffer chronic back and joint damage, long-term disability, and there is still almost no real alternative to protect them.

Rub‑X is the world’s first automated harvesting system specifically designed for natural rubber. It holds international patents and is listed on the UN WIPO GREEN platform. It operates with ultra-low cost, ultra-low energy, on any terrain—fundamentally safeguarding workers’ health.

Yet, despite broad international recognition, the inventor has not received the support he deserves. On the contrary, because Rub‑X disrupts entrenched supply chain interests and industry inertia, efforts to secure pilot collaborations, funding, and global partnerships have repeatedly stalled. The inventor has even lost his means of livelihood, bearing heavy financial and psychological burdens—simply for trying to solve a serious problem long overlooked by the world.

A call for WIPO and related agencies to fulfill their promises

WIPO, UNDP, FAO and other international bodies have long declared in their charters and SDG initiatives that they:

  • support innovation,
  • protect workers,
  • and promote human rights and sustainability.

Now is the time for them to honor these commitments and take up their responsibility:

  • By providing custodianship, pilot validation, and fair funding channels for technologies that aim to protect vulnerable workers and drive sustainable industry — ensuring such breakthroughs are not blocked or buried.
  • By guaranteeing that those who genuinely address global pain points are not left isolated, or effectively punished, for doing so.

Because if those who solve our problems are met with indifference, obstruction, or punishment, who will dare keep searching for answers for this world?
